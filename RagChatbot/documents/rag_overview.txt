Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is a technique that combines a retrieval
system with a language model. Instead of relying only on what the model
learned during training, RAG first searches a knowledge base for relevant
passages, then feeds those passages to the language model as context. This
grounds the model's answers in real, up-to-date documents and reduces
hallucination.

A typical RAG pipeline has three stages:
1. Ingestion: documents are split into chunks and converted into vector
   embeddings, which are stored in a vector index (e.g. FAISS).
2. Retrieval: when a user asks a question, the question is embedded and the
   most similar chunks are retrieved from the index.
3. Generation: the retrieved chunks are combined with the question into a
   prompt, which is sent to a language model to produce the final answer.

RAG is widely used for chatbots that need to answer questions about private
or domain-specific documents, such as internal company wikis, research
papers, or legal documents, without retraining the underlying model.
